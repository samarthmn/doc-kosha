Answers to the questions people ask while deciding whether DocKosha fits. Every
answer here matches the shipped implementation; where a capability is limited,
the limit is stated rather than glossed.

## Documents, formats, and limits

### What file types can I upload?

Four categories, validated against a fixed list at upload time:

- **Text/docs:** `pdf`, `pptx`, `docx`, `xlsx`, `xlsm`, `csv`, `md`
- **Images:** `jpg`, `jpeg`, `png`, `webp`
- **Video:** `mp4`, `mov`, `avi`, `mkv`, `webm`
- **Audio:** `mp3`, `wav`, `ogg`, `aac`

Anything else is rejected with an unsupported-file-type error. Free workspaces
are narrower still: PDF uploads only.

### Are there size limits?

Yes, and they are processing limits rather than plan limits:

- A conversion accepts at most **50 MiB** of input.
- PDF processing — watermarking, redaction, page counting — accepts at most
  **100 MiB**.
- A merged PDF download accepts at most **100 MiB** of combined input.
- A data room ZIP download covers at most **100 documents** and **500 MiB** of
  combined input.

Separately, your plan caps total workspace storage (250 MB on Free, up to
200 GB on Max) and, on Free, public bandwidth at 2 GB per month.

### Which files get converted to PDF?

Mandatory DocYantra `0.0.25` converts supported CSV, Markdown, and Office files to PDF within its documented envelope. PDFs are already PDFs. Images, video, and audio
are delivered in their original form and are never converted.

Conversion gives watermarking and per-page analytics a consistent target when
DocYantra completes it. The viewer prefers the converted PDF
**only** after successful conversion.

### What happens when a conversion fails?

Conversion fails closed. Outside its validated format, size, and fidelity
envelope, the processing engine refuses the job and returns a stable typed
reason code — it never emits a degraded or partial render, and never silently
drops visible content such as an image it could not decode.

When that happens, viewing is not blocked: the viewer falls back to the
**original uploaded file**. A viewer opening a `.docx` whose conversion failed
receives that `.docx`, not a half-rendered PDF.

<Callout type="warn">
  Watermarking is the one place where fallback is not offered. If a download
  requires a watermark and watermarking cannot be completed, the download fails
  rather than serving unwatermarked bytes.
</Callout>

The failure codes, their meanings, and which ones are worth retrying are listed
in [Troubleshooting](/docs/reference/troubleshooting).

### Is my document sent to a third-party conversion service?

DocYantra is the mandatory direct dependency and runs the documented conversion path. It controls its own bundled dependencies and fails closed outside its validated envelope.

## Viewers and access

### Do viewers need an account?

No. Anyone opening a shared document link or data room link is anonymous to the
system unless a gate on that link asks for identity. There is no sign-up, and no
password to create.

An email OTP gate, an allow/blocklist, or an NDA gate will ask a viewer for an
email address and verify it with a one-time code — but that is verification, not
account creation.

Teammates are different. People you invite **into your workspace** — with
document or data room access levels — do sign in, because they are members of
your workspace rather than viewers of a link.

### How do the gates combine?

They combine with AND. Every gate configured on a link must pass, and every one
of them is enforced on the server before any document bytes are released. The
server evaluates them in a fixed order, which determines only which message a
viewer sees first:

1. **Link and workspace exist and are entitled.** Otherwise the response is a
   plain "not found" — an unentitled workspace's links are indistinguishable
   from links that never existed.
2. **Host.** If the request arrives on a custom domain, that domain must be
   verified, active, entitled, and owned by the same workspace.
3. **Email identity.** Required whenever email verification, an NDA gate, or an
   allow/blocklist is active on the link.
4. **Allow/blocklist.** The verified email is checked against the link's list.
5. **NDA.** A signature must exist for that email _and_ its signed PDF must be
   ready.
6. **Password.** Requested, then verified.
7. **Bandwidth.** A workspace over its bandwidth allowance stops serving public
   views.
8. **Link state.** Expired or revoked links are refused during resolution.

On success, a one-time-open link is consumed immediately: it is revoked, so the
next open is refused as revoked.

### Can a viewer forward the link to someone else?

If the link has no email gate, yes — a URL is a URL. That is why the gates
exist. An allow/blocklist plus an email OTP gate limits access to addresses you
name; a dynamic watermark stamps the viewer's identity into the delivered PDF
bytes; and the viewer log records the opens, with a verified email attached
whenever the link required one.

### Does screenshot protection stop screenshots?

No, and the product says so in the viewer itself. Screenshot protection is a
**best-effort deterrent**: it reacts to tab blur, window focus loss, and a few
known screenshot hotkeys by covering the content. A browser cannot block
operating-system-level screen capture — on Windows, for example, `PrintScreen`
events reach the page only _after_ the OS has already captured the screen — and
nothing in a browser can stop a phone camera pointed at the monitor.

Treat it as friction against casual capture. If a document genuinely must not
leave the room, rely on dynamic watermarking and the audit log for
accountability, not on the shield for prevention.

### Are watermarks drawn in the viewer?

No. Watermarks are **burned into the delivered PDF bytes on the server** before
those bytes reach the browser. There is no browser overlay to remove with dev
tools, and no unwatermarked copy is served alongside the watermarked one.

Images, audio, and video keep their original delivery, because there is no PDF
watermark representation for them. That applies even to older links where the
watermark flag is already switched on. See
[Watermark in bytes](/docs/how-it-works/watermark-in-bytes).

## Analytics and privacy

### What viewer analytics are collected?

Four core events, recorded server-side for entitled workspaces:

| Event          | Meaning                       |
| -------------- | ----------------------------- |
| `view`         | A link was opened             |
| `download`     | A download was taken          |
| `page_view`    | Time spent on a specific page |
| `section_time` | Time spent in a media section |

Viewer-submitted `feedback` and `qa` events are recorded when those surfaces are
enabled on the link. Country is derived server-side on the `view` event and kept
only as aggregated counts. Viewer analytics are pruned at 365 days.

Collection and presentation are separate questions. Viewer analytics,
page-level events, country aggregates, media sections, and the shipped export
surfaces are ordinary application capabilities. Cloud plans may limit hosted
capacity without creating a second source edition.

### What is deliberately not collected?

- **Raw IP addresses are never stored.** Country is derived at request time; the
  address itself is not persisted.
- **Viewer email is only captured when the link's own settings require it** —
  an OTP gate, an allow/blocklist, or an NDA gate. It is only persisted into
  analytics when the link's "collect email for analytics" setting is on.
- Viewer session tracking uses httpOnly, per-link cookies. Local storage and
  ad-hoc tracking cookies are not used for viewer identity.

### How does consent work?

DocKosha's own marketing and product analytics — the third-party vendors on
dockosha.com and in the authenticated app — are consent-gated and regional:

- **EU/EEA/UK visitors, and visitors whose region cannot be determined, get
  nothing until they explicitly opt in.** Unknown geography fails safe to EU
  behaviour.
- **Elsewhere, analytics are default-granted with a persistent opt-out** in the
  consent banner and in settings.
- **Global Privacy Control always wins.** A GPC signal forces analytics and
  session replay off regardless of region or of any stored consent.

Session replay only runs when analytics consent is granted, and it masks inputs
and blocks sensitive surfaces — document viewers, media players, upload
controls, password and payment fields — so document content is never recorded.

First-party viewer analytics on shared links are a different system: they are
the product feature your recipients' engagement data comes from, they run
server-side for entitled workspaces, and they follow the storage discipline
above (no raw IPs, email only when the link requires it). They are disclosed in
the privacy policy rather than gated behind the marketing consent banner. See
[Privacy and analytics](/docs/how-it-works/privacy-and-analytics).

## Self-hosting, Cloud, and providers

### What is the difference between self-hosting and Cloud?

The source is public under AGPL-3.0-or-later for reading and contribution, but
external self-hosting is not currently available. The mandatory direct
DocYantra `0.0.25` dependency is private and unavailable to external users.
DocKosha Cloud is the available hosted product.

### Can I install DocKosha or obtain DocYantra access?

Not currently. There is no external DocYantra package, token, SDK, API,
standalone product, or engine-license offering. Use DocKosha Cloud for the
available product.

### How does document processing work?

DocYantra is the mandatory direct `0.0.25` dependency. Operations outside its
validated conversion and redaction envelope return explicit typed errors; there
is no provider-selection variable or fallback. DocKosha Cloud may operate the
same engine for managed processing, but it is not a separate install
requirement.

### Where is the authoritative capability list?

The [capability matrix](/docs/reference/capability-matrix). It is rendered here
from the same `docs/capability-matrix.md` file that ships in the repository, so
there is exactly one copy of those facts.
