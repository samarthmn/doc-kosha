Two link settings decide what a viewer can take away: **Allow downloads** and **Screenshot protection**. One of them is a real control. The other is a deterrent, and this page says so plainly.

## Allow downloads

Downloads are decided per link, and enforced on the server. New links start with downloads **off**.

1. Create or edit the link.
2. Turn **Allow downloads** on or off.
3. Save.

With downloads off, the download controls are absent from the viewer and the download endpoints refuse the request. With downloads on, a viewer can take:

- The document they are currently viewing.
- In a data room: the **whole room** as a ZIP, or the **folder they are currently in** as a ZIP.

Downloads in a data room respect [access lists](/docs/guides/access-lists). A viewer's ZIP contains only what their rules grant them.

## What a viewer actually receives

| Link configuration                                                    | What is delivered                                                                                                                                                        |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Downloads on, watermark off                                           | The **original uploaded file**, unchanged.                                                                                                                               |
| Downloads on, watermark on, PDF or document with completed conversion | A **PDF with the watermark burned into its bytes** — the original PDF, or the PDF produced when DocYantra completed conversion. The filename is forced to end in `.pdf`. |
| Downloads on, watermark on, image / audio / video                     | The **original file**. These formats have no PDF watermark representation, so the watermark setting does not change what is delivered.                                   |

### Watermarked downloads fail closed

When a link requires a watermark, DocKosha will not fall back to unmarked bytes to make a download succeed.

- A **single-file download** whose required watermark cannot be produced returns an error. The viewer does not get the file.
- A **ZIP download** omits any file whose required watermark failed, and adds a `_MISSING_FILES.txt` entry inside the archive listing what was left out and suggesting the viewer retry or contact the owner.

That is the intended behaviour: a missing file is recoverable, an unmarked leak is not.

See [Watermarking](/docs/guides/watermarking) for how to configure the mark itself.

### Other reasons a download can be refused

- The workspace has exhausted its monthly public download bandwidth cap on the Cloud Free plan.
- The Cloud subscription lapsed, in which case the link stops resolving entirely.
- The viewer's [access rules](/docs/guides/access-lists) do not grant that document.

## Screenshot protection

Screenshot protection is a **best-effort deterrent**. Turn it on to discourage casual capture. Do not plan around it as a control.

New links have it **on** by default.

### What it actually does

When enabled, the viewer wraps the document in a shield that blurs the content in two situations:

- **The tab loses focus or is hidden.** Switching windows, minimising, or moving to another tab blurs the document until focus returns. Many capture workflows involve leaving the page, so this catches some of them.
- **A known screenshot hotkey is pressed.** `PrintScreen`, and `Cmd+Shift+3 / 4 / 5 / S`, trigger a blur for about a second and a half.

While the shield is up, the viewer sees a panel that says screenshot protection is enabled and states the limitation.

### What it cannot do — stated plainly

**Browsers cannot block operating-system screen capture.** No web page can. A browser tab is not a trusted display surface, and web APIs give a page no ability to suppress, intercept, or deny a capture performed by the operating system.

Concretely:

- On **Windows**, the `PrintScreen` key event reaches the page _after_ the operating system has already captured the screen. The blur that follows is theatre with respect to that specific capture — the image already exists.
- On **macOS**, the screenshot hotkeys are handled by the system; the page can observe the keystroke, not veto the capture.
- **Dedicated capture tools** — Snipping Tool, Screenshot, `scrot`, and every screen recorder — never touch the page at all.
- **Phone cameras** are entirely outside the browser.
- **Virtual machines, remote desktop sessions, and external capture cards** capture the display, not the page.
- **Browser extensions, developer tools, and print-to-PDF** operate at a level the page cannot police.

Screenshot protection raises the effort required for a casual grab. It does not prevent a determined one, and it should never be described to a counterparty as if it did.

It also only covers the in-browser viewer. Once a file has been downloaded, the shield is irrelevant — the viewer has the bytes. If downloads are on, screenshot protection is close to meaningless for that link.

### What to use instead, or alongside

If the real requirement is "I need to know who leaked this", use controls that survive capture and produce evidence:

- **[Dynamic watermarks](/docs/guides/watermarking)** — carry the viewer's verified email, IP, and timestamp. The lines are burned into the delivered PDF and are also drawn over the pages on screen, so a photograph of the monitor carries the attribution too. This is the most useful control on this page.
- **[NDA gates](/docs/guides/nda-gates)** — get agreement on the record before access.
- **[Access lists](/docs/guides/access-lists)** — do not show people what they do not need. Content never delivered cannot be captured.
- **[Expiry](/docs/guides/gate-expiry) and [one-time open](/docs/guides/gate-one-time-open)** — shrink the window.
- **[Audit log](/docs/guides/audit-log) and [analytics](/docs/guides/analytics)** — record who opened what, and when.

## Recommended combinations

| Situation                           | Suggested settings                                                         |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Reviewers must read only            | Downloads off, screenshot protection on, watermark on with the email line. |
| Counterparty needs their own copy   | Downloads on, watermark on with email and date-time, NDA gate on.          |
| Broad distribution, low sensitivity | Downloads on, no watermark, expiry set.                                    |
